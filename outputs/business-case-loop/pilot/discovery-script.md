# Business discovery: scope-change decision preparation

Prepared for review. No outreach sent or interviews conducted.

## Who qualifies for this test

Owner or delivery lead handling fixed-scope client work, with multiple ongoing projects, recent changes communicated in text, and authority to test a small service. Team size is a targeting hypothesis, not a qualification substitute. Require a concrete recent episode and a permissioned/redacted source packet. No accessible buyer is currently established.

## Twenty-minute interview

1. Walk me through the last client request that changed an agreed project. What was agreed, what arrived, and what happened next?
2. Did you notice the difference immediately? If yes, what stopped you acting on it?
3. Which work consumed time: finding the agreement, interpreting it, estimating impact, obtaining approval, discussing it with the client, or recording the decision?
4. Show the brief/request/change record if you can share it. What important context is missing from the written record?
5. How often has this happened in the last month? Use actual examples rather than guesses where possible.
6. What did it cost in review time or rework? Separate measured hours from estimates and goodwill work chosen intentionally.
7. How do you handle it today? What does your project tool, shared log, assistant or change-control process already do well?
8. Who decides whether a new tool/service is worth paying for? What approval is required?
9. Would you provide one redacted case for a comparison against your actual current process? Who would judge the result?
10. After showing a reviewed sample: what decision would this have changed? What would still require rereading the full history?

Do not ask “Would you use an AI tool?” and count a positive answer as validation. Log objections verbatim. Avoid pitching before understanding the actual failure.

## Behavioral evidence ladder

Anecdote < concrete past example < permitted source packet < scheduled pilot with decision owner < actual paid pilot < repeat paid usage. A declined price is evidence, not a reason to relabel a free trial as willingness to pay. No stage has yet been achieved with actual customers.

## Proposed gates, not validated thresholds

For the first five qualified interviews: continue only if at least three show repeated preparation/recordkeeping failures and at least two provide a usable packet. If most knowingly absorb extra work and preparation is trivial, reject this problem framing.

For any pilot: establish the usual preparation time first, including search and source verification. Compare total assisted time including input preparation, checking and corrections. Stop if the service creates more work, cannot establish source authority, or confidently treats acknowledgment as approval.

After two completed pilots: ask for a concrete paid continuation at an explicit scoped offer. If neither pays or commits through its real purchasing process, do not scale or declare demand validated. Investigate whether failure is price, value, adoption, trust or sample selection before one bounded revision of the offer.

Small numbers are discovery screens, not statistical proof of a market. No sales uplift or recovered revenue claim follows from a favorable interview.
