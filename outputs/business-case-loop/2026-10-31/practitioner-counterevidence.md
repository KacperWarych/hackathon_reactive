# Practitioner anecdotes and counterevidence

Retrieved 2026-10-31. Public Reddit comments are unverified, self-selected anecdotes, possibly promotional. They are discovery leads, not representative market research or customer validation. No commenters were contacted. Unsupported numerical claims in original posts are deliberately excluded.

## What changed the hypothesis

A discussion of agency scope processes includes one commenter describing an existing Monday-based process with AI comparing project plans and call summaries against agreed scope, with a CTO making the final decision. Another describes a client-visible change tracker and says relationship pressure, rather than lack of detection, is the harder problem. This directly challenges a claim that scope-change detection is novel or sufficient. We have not verified either implementation or its performance.

Source: https://www.reddit.com/r/agencynewbies/comments/1rxu0se/agency_owners_honest_question_how_do_you_actually/

A separate developer discussion includes a shared change log, routing requests through a PM, explicit scope and add-on pricing, and delaying acceptance until review. These are plausible low-cost alternatives. The original poster is also doing research, so the existence of the post itself should not be counted as buyer demand.

Source: https://www.reddit.com/r/webdev/comments/1r2o1mw/scope_creep_do_you_have_a_system_or_are_you_just/

## Implication

Interview separately for missed detection, expensive reconstruction/preparation, weak approval tracking, and intentional concessions. A tool that flags known extra work will not by itself solve a deliberate commercial choice. The proposed paid pilot therefore prepares a source-backed decision packet and maintains a reviewed log; it does not promise recovered margin or automatic extra billing. This is still an unvalidated offer and competes with a simple shared document.

Official Productive material already describes budgeting/profitability and scenario planning. This establishes adjacent paid functionality, not willingness to pay for our standalone service. Its broader bundle is a substitution risk; current prices should not be transplanted into our offer.
