# Broader non-sales workflows — 2026-10-31

## Recommendation and evidence limits

Reopen the search rather than assume sales is the winner. The most promising non-sales experiments are **fixed-fee service-delivery change-request triage** and **technical-support escalation handover with a record of what was tried and contradicted**. They offer concrete decisions, textual inputs, chronological reversals, and small replay demos. They are hypotheses, not validated opportunities. Of these, agency delivery has the more plausible low-friction path to a first pilot; support has stronger recurring workflow fit but harder incumbent competition and access requirements.

Read `outputs/business-case-loop/latest.md`. Reuse means report-bound text append, manual **Save entry and refresh report**, evidence retrieval, Reactor answer revision, general version history/diff, and citations. It does not mean autonomous monitoring, semantic state tracking, semantic confirmation, or enforced customer isolation. Customer metadata loss requires isolated input histories or a fix before all multi-entity demos. A generalized report prompt is not a finished domain product. English-only confirmation logic must not become a claim about Polish or other-language resolution.

Primary-source research below verifies workflows and vendor features as of retrieval on 2026-10-31. It does not establish pain frequency, user satisfaction, willingness to pay, or vendor feature quality. Proposed distribution routes and data availability are assumptions to validate, not existing access. No outreach was sent, no app was edited, and no real customer validation occurred.

## Primary-source competitive checks

- Productive describes scope-change handling and keeping out-of-scope work in a separate budget. Its help documentation supports modifying budget scenarios and applying selected changes. This establishes a nearby delivery workflow and a non-AI alternative; it does not prove it detects unstructured requests automatically. [Productive scope management](https://productive.io/blog/scope-creep-in-project-management/), [Budget scenarios](https://help.productive.io/en/articles/10296200-managing-and-applying-scenarios-to-deals-and-budgets)
- Intercom Copilot uses conversation/ticket notes and prior history. Its FAQ says historical conversations are ingested daily, with selected conversations from the last four months. Its product page places Copilot inside the operational inbox and connects existing helpdesk context and tools. A generic support-memory product has substantial overlap; the history ingestion rule is not evidence that current-case context is stale. [Copilot FAQ](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/9121381-copilot-faqs), [Copilot product](https://www.intercom.com/helpdesk/copilot)
- SAP's quotation comparison app supports revisions, version history, proposed delivery dates and associated notes/documents. Ariba markets AI bid summaries and scenario comparisons. “Updated supplier comparison” alone is not a gap. [SAP quotation comparison](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/af9ef57f504840d2b81be8667206d485/0fd1842b7cb14be4bc38e43d457994dd.html), [Ariba sourcing features](https://www.sap.com/products/spend-management/ariba-sourcing/features.html)
- Jira Delivery Agent automatically monitors project progress, identifies stale or blocked work and slipping deadlines, and can operate in automation rules. This directly weakens a generic reactive project-status pitch. [Jira Delivery Agent](https://support.atlassian.com/rovo/docs/work-with-jira-delivery-agent/)
- Atlassian's incident-response handbook uses an incident timeline and formal communications. Its AI PIR feature generates a reviewable incident summary and can use an associated Slack channel. PIR generation is retrospective; it does not establish equivalence to every live handover need. [Incident response workflow](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/handbook/incident-response), [AI post-incident review](https://support.atlassian.com/jira-service-management-cloud/docs/create-a-post-incident-review-using-atlassian-intelligence/)
- Procore supports change events originating in RFIs, emails, meetings and correspondence, and downstream quotes/change orders. This is an established workflow, not a newly discovered gap. ## Deep test A: fixed-fee service-delivery change-request triage

**Precise job:** Before a team starts the next requested item, let its PM see whether the request potentially changes an agreed deliverable and which clarification or human estimate is needed. Start with small web/software agencies, not contractual/legal adjudication or automated billing. The user is a delivery PM; the plausible budget holder is an owner or delivery director responsible for margin. Do not claim legal entitlement or decide that work is chargeable from conversational language alone.

**Event and decision:** Original brief says “lead form; no CRM integration.” A later client note asks for two-way CRM sync. The report highlights the conflict with both sources and asks whether to replace an existing deliverable or estimate additional scope. A later approved change note alters the working baseline; a casual “sounds good” or internal developer enthusiasm does not. A backdated note is evidence about the earlier state, not automatically a newer approval.

**Why reactive:** A new request changes what the team should do *before* work starts. Reactive delivery can prevent delay in noticing a changed instruction. Yet a manual refresh before planning may be sufficient at low volume. Stateful Reactor value remains unproven; compare with event-triggered stateless retrieval on identical evidence. A new report for every message would create noise.

**Reuse and gaps:** Existing dated evidence, report revisions, general diff and manual entry support a demonstrable sequence. Required additions are source-type and project identity, approved-baseline provenance, proposal versus authorized-change distinction, semantic change records and review status. Do not estimate effort/cost from prose without an explicit estimator's input. The code's quote checks do not validate approval authority. Real deployment would need inbox/task integration, permissions and material-change suppression.

**Smallest honest demo:** One explicitly synthetic project, five textual events: baseline; in-scope copy correction; out-of-scope integration request; ambiguous acknowledgment; explicit approved scope substitution. Use the current manual save-and-refresh flow. At every step show what changed, relevant original text, unresolved ambiguity and a proposed question. Add a second-project decoy and out-of-order event. Avoid inventing a cost, contractual ruling or completed automatic notification. This tests revision mechanics, not demand.

**Data access:** For a genuine pilot ask one agency for two completed, consented projects: initial brief, dated requests, accepted changes, and the team's final interpretation. Request only the minimum necessary text with unrelated client data removed. Availability is plausible but unconfirmed; approvals may have happened verbally or in inaccessible chats. If authoritative scope cannot be reconstructed, the right output is uncertainty, not a confident scope label.

**Distribution hypothesis:** Owner-led agencies can potentially adopt a manual-upload pilot without a system-wide rollout. Reach via existing professional relationships or agency/PM communities only after outreach authorization. Sell an evaluated workflow artifact first; a plugin marketplace/inbox integration comes after observed repeated usage. No distribution advantage is established from the user being a developer.

**Alternatives:** PM plus change log; Productive budgets/scenarios; task templates requiring scope approval; a general LLM with the whole project brief; stateless RAG. The strongest competitor may be making the current change-control process explicit.

**Proposed kill criteria:** In five interviews, fewer than three can show a repeated missed/ambiguous scope change with a concrete consequence; data is too fragmented to identify an accepted baseline; two pilot users find a checklist equally effective; or review effort exceeds saved effort. For a 20-event labeled replay, reject the release if it labels any deliberately ambiguous acknowledgment as authorized scope change or mixes projects. These small-sample gates are development rules, not production reliability claims. Business pilot target: reviewer-confirmed usefulness on at least 80% of surfaced potential changes and at least 25% less triage time versus current practice. Thresholds are proposals to pre-register, not measured results.

## Deep test B: unresolved technical-support escalation handover

**Precise job:** Help a receiving technical-support agent determine what has already been tried, what the latest result rules out, and which missing information should be requested next. Start with a single non-critical SaaS case handed from tier 1 to tier 2. User: receiving agent; buyer: support lead. This is a reviewed case brief, not autonomous customer replies or technical remediation.

**Event and decision:** Agent suggests cache clearing. Customer reports no improvement; a later diagnostic shows failure affects a second browser but only one workspace. The brief must retain the failed attempt, revise the reproduction conditions and avoid recommending the same cache step as new. A later “works on my personal workspace” does not mean the customer's affected workspace is fixed. A new failure after a claimed fix reopens investigation.

**Why reactive:** New test outcomes can invalidate a previous hypothesis or proposed next step. A brief updated before escalation/shift handover may reduce repeated questions and stale assumptions. Automatic response to every message is unnecessary unless measured delay has consequences; the meaningful trigger is new diagnostic evidence or transfer of ownership.

**Reuse and gaps:** Existing excerpts, dates, manual append-refresh and versions fit. Missing: stable case/contact/workspace identity, attempted-action and outcome tuples, attachment/log ingestion, status semantics, agent corrections, handling multiple parallel issues and a completeness check. Bounded top-k may drop an old failed attempt; a report claiming “all steps tried” is not justified. An extractive list must expose its coverage or use exhaustive case processing.

**Smallest honest demo:** An explicitly synthetic six-event ticket containing initial symptom, attempted workaround, failed result, contradictory environment detail, misleading “thanks,” and reopened symptom. Show dated attempted/observed/unknown fields with quotes and a draft internal escalation note. Add a second case and an older failed attempt separated by unrelated messages. Measure unsupported resolution, repeated failed advice, missed diagnostic updates and source correctness. Do not claim a proven diagnosis from text alone.

**Data access:** A support leader could export anonymized closed multi-message cases plus existing escalation notes; this is not available by default. Personal data, product secrets and logs may limit access. Public issue threads can test mechanics but are not representative substitutes for support validation. An initial pilot should remain within one case and explicitly permitted export; CRM/helpdesk integrations are a later cost.

**Distribution hypothesis:** Start with a support team handling repeated tier-1/tier-2 transfers where the incumbent summary demonstrably loses attempted/outcome detail. A generic “better summaries” pitch is weak against built-in Copilot. Distribution via helpdesk app marketplaces would require connectors, support and permissions, so do not count it as immediate reach. An internal export comparison can precede deployment.

**Alternatives:** The existing support escalation template, Intercom Copilot, an exhaustive full-thread summary using a general model, saved macros and stateless RAG. Compare against the actual tool the buyer has, not a deliberately weak last-message baseline.

**Proposed kill criteria:** Users cannot identify repeated handover failures; incumbent configuration solves the problem; agents must reread the full ticket to trust every output; there are too few handovers to justify a separate workflow; or restricted data makes a representative pilot impossible. Replay release gate: no cross-case evidence, no unsupported “resolved” status, and no repeated failed step presented as a fresh unqualified suggestion in the challenge set. Business pilot target: at least 25% reduction in receiving-agent prep time with non-inferior next-question quality and no increase in missed prior attempts. Proposed, unmeasured thresholds.

## Cross-cutting attacks and decision rule

1. **Is it a decision or a summary?** Require the reviewer to name what action changed after the new source. If only wording changes, reactivity adds little value.
2. **Does persistent state help?** Compare manual preparation, on-demand full-thread analysis, event-triggered stateless RAG and Reactor with matched inputs. Attribute gains separately to retrieval, refresh timing and persistent state.
3. **Can users trust coverage?** Old obligations, failed steps and baseline clauses can be absent from top-k retrieval. Retrieval similarity and source existence are not semantic correctness or completeness.
4. **Who pays and why now?** A reachable buyer, repeated event, measurable manual cost and permission to access inputs matter more than narrative novelty. Require real examples before building connectors.
5. **Can incumbents absorb it?** All eight categories have adjacent or direct tooling. A viable wedge needs observed dissatisfaction with a specific job and a lower-friction path to solving it, not an unverified missing-feature claim.
6. **Can the pilot falsify it?** Include unchanged notes, contradictory notes, ambiguous acceptance, backdated records and cross-entity decoys. Record reviewer disagreement rather than hiding it in a single accuracy number.

Do not rank these above sales on invented market scores. Run the same short discovery/replay protocol for agency delivery, technical support and sales; choose the one with accessible evidence, repeated costly decisions and observed improvement. Stop expanding the domain list when a real pilot can resolve those uncertainties.

## Demo quality, reactive proof, and business prospect are separate

| Candidate | Demo quality | Strongest reactive proof | Adoption/payment uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency scope triage | High with a compact baseline/request/approval sequence | A late accepted substitution changes the baseline without treating casual agreement as authorization | Owners may value margin control but resist another inbox; a PM checklist could solve the real problem. Require repeated voluntary use on new requests, then a concrete paid-continuation decision, not a positive demo reaction. |
| Support escalation | High with a failed workaround and reopening | Preserve old attempts and revise the next question when new evidence contradicts them | Support leaders already pay for integrated assistants; a detached brief adds copy/paste and another data processor. Demonstrate incremental improvement over the deployed incumbent and ask for an integration/pilot owner before building. |
| Engineering decision invalidation (additional challenger) | High when a new technical finding invalidates a documented assumption | Identify exactly which earlier decision depends on the overturned assumption, retaining unaffected decisions | Could be valuable technical proof without a standalone budget or habitual workflow. Generic risk updates overlap Jira; dependency semantics and a trustworthy decision graph need separate implementation. Requires direct customer discovery and additional domain-specific competitor research before a market recommendation. |

A paid pilot can be proposed after usable evidence exists; its price should be established with the buyer and actual cost, not invented here. If interviewees praise the output but will not supply representative data, designate a reviewer, or use it on the next live case, treat that as weak adoption evidence. If a trial saves time but no budget holder will authorize paid continuation, revisit buyer, packaging and alternative costs rather than calling the business validated.
