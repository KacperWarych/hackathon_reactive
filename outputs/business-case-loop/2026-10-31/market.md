## Recommendation

Test a **buyer-confirmed blocker change brief for an individual active sales opportunity**, potentially with Polish-speaking B2B service or software sellers whose customer conversations span multiple calls, after fixing identity isolation and validating Polish resolution semantics. On each appended conversation, show what changed, which requirements or objections remain open, and the next question the seller should ask; attach the previous and new source quotes. Treat this as a product hypothesis, not established demand or demonstrated model accuracy.

This is the strongest intersection of (1) reactive state revision, (2) an identifiable commercial workflow, and (3) reuse of the current implementation. A new summarization dashboard or generic “AI sales assistant” has weak differentiation. The potentially valuable event is a **change in a buyer's decision conditions**, especially a reversal or a falsely assumed resolution.

## Competitive findings and testable gaps

**A — Gong overlaps directly.** Gong's AI Deal Monitor continuously tracks opportunity activity and produces warnings, including red flags and stalled deals. A product merely detecting deal risk is already covered. [Gong AI Deal Monitor](https://help.gong.io/docs/understanding-ai-deal-monitor)

The concrete narrower contrast is status semantics: Gong documents that its email red-flag warning disappears after a reply or when the sender joins a call. Its documentation also says that red-flag warnings are currently English-only and that foreign-language emails are not considered for its pricing warning. An explicit, cited buyer confirmation before marking a blocker resolved is therefore a defensible **experiment**, as is Polish-language handling. Neither is an established project capability: the audit finds English-only resolution validation, and exact quoted text does not establish semantic resolution. A Polish pitch requires its own labeled evaluation and validator changes. The Gong warning is an attention/workflow signal, not necessarily a claim that the buyer objection is resolved; treating the two as equivalent would be misleading. This does not prove Gong lacks equivalent blocker-tracking capabilities elsewhere or that this project handles either case well. [Gong warning settings](https://help.gong.io/docs/customize-your-deal-warning-settings)

**B — Gainsight overlaps directly.** Gainsight lists account health narratives, renewal/risk summaries, meeting action items, and conversational access to account information. Its Copilot can also create/update Timeline, CTA, Success Plan and Task records. Competing as a generic renewal summary or task creator is not sufficient. [Gainsight AI features](https://support.gainsight.com/Gainsight_AI/AI_in_Gainsight_Products/Customer_Success_%28CS%29/AI_Features_in_Customer_Success_%28CS%29_Product), [Copilot overview](https://support.gainsight.com/gainsight_nxt/AI_Assistants/Copilot/About/Copilot_Overview)

Its FAQ documents a 50-most-recent-Timeline-entries-per-account limit and a default one-year retrieval window. A long-history promise ledger could be a testable angle, but bounded top-k retrieval in our project must first prove it retains old still-open commitments. Current documentation alone cannot support a superiority claim. [Gainsight Copilot FAQ](https://support.gainsight.com/gainsight_nxt/AI_Assistants/Copilot/FAQs/FAQs_for_Copilot)

**C — Dovetail overlaps directly.** Dovetail's current Channels 2.0 beta consolidates feedback into ranked ideas with original evidence, uses business context including ARR, accepts sales conversations, and supports owners and downstream actions. A continuously updated evidence-backed feedback report is therefore not novel. A small-data, explicit hypothesis-reversal workflow might still be useful, but differentiation and buyer preference need direct comparison. Avoid relying on older cached Channels 1.0 descriptions. [Dovetail Channels 2.0 documentation](https://docs.dovetail.com/help/channels)

## Proposed next experiments

These are proposed decision rules, not achieved metrics or industry benchmarks.

1. **Buyer problem interviews:** recruit five target sellers who handle multi-call deals. Ask them to reconstruct their last changed or falsely resolved customer objection using artifacts; measure current prep effort and consequences. Proceed only if at least three show concrete repeated examples and two offer a consented pilot history. Do not send outreach without user authorization.
2. **Blind longitudinal replay:** use 20 consenting customer histories with at least two interactions, or explicitly synthetic fixtures until real data is available. Have human reviewers label open, resolved, reopened, superseded and unknown blockers before showing AI output. Replay each addition chronologically. Compare the existing Q&A, a last-call summary, and the proposed updated brief.
3. **Hard counterexamples:** seller says “we can fix it” without buyer agreement; buyer says “yes” to a different question; old blocker remains open across many unrelated calls; buyer reopens a previously resolved blocker; note corrects a date; two customers use similar language. Score false resolutions separately and require zero on the small release gate before a wider pilot; passing this is not a population safety guarantee.
4. **Reactivity test:** replay non-material notes as well as meaningful changes. Measure useful change precision, missed material changes, and duplicated alerts. Proposed pilot target: at least 90% reviewer-accepted change briefs and at least 30% median prep-time reduction versus the current workflow, without increased false resolutions. Establish sample sizes and uncertainty before claiming success.
5. **Commercial test:** show actual artifacts to pilot buyers and request a paid continuation or explicit pricing objection. Favor recorded buying behavior over enthusiasm. Do not invent a price or TAM from these sources.
